## 1. Selling into the NHS in 2026

The NHS still buys a lot from outside its own walls: £24.1bn of healthcare from non-NHS bodies in 2024-25, according to the National Audit Office.

But almost everything about how that money is spent has changed in the last 18 months.

Integrated care boards are cutting headcount by half. NHS England is being folded back into the Department of Health. The Procurement Act now governs most purchasing, while clinical services follow the Provider Selection Regime, where a well-performing incumbent can be re-awarded without competition. And the national Health Systems Support Framework is no longer starting new call-offs.

For health businesses, and especially overseas health tech companies looking at the UK, four things matter most:

1. Know which rulebook applies to you
2. Arrive with your assurance pack done: DTAC, clinical safety, data security
3. Be honest about your evidence against NICE’s standards
4. Sell a specific trust or ICB problem that links to a funded national priority

## 2. The business of UK sport, 2026

Premier League clubs earned a record £6.8bn in 2024/25. The more interesting number is where the growth came from.

Matchday revenue rose 15%. Commercial rose 13%. Broadcast, still the biggest line, grew just 2%.

Women’s Super League revenue grew 39% to £90m, and women’s sport drew a record 397 million hours of UK TV viewing in 2025.

Meanwhile 64.6% of adults in England are now active, the highest in a decade, but the gap between the most and least affluent is widening.

The money in sport is moving towards fans, partners, women’s sport and flexible ways of taking part. And it is concentrating as it moves.

For governing bodies, clubs and sports businesses, that means three things: build the direct relationship with your audience, sell partnerships on outcomes, and design for how people actually take part now.

## 3. Where sport meets health

England is more active than at any point in a decade. 64.6% of adults now meet the activity guideline.

But the gains are going to people who were already likely to move. 73.2% of the most affluent adults are active, against 53.8% of the least affluent. And 28.4% of children do less than 30 minutes a day.

At the same time, 456,532 adults are waiting for community musculoskeletal care in England, almost half of everyone on a community health waiting list.

The most valuable ground sits between sport and the NHS: pathways that take people from MSK rehabilitation into lasting activity, designed for the people who are not already active, and measured in waits avoided and absence reduced.
